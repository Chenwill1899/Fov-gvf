# Claim-First Scientific Writing

Advance the claim directly. State what the method does, what the text argues, and what the evidence shows.

## Writing rules

1. Lead each paragraph with its main point.
2. Give each paragraph one job.
3. Use positive scope: state what the work studies, tests, explains, or supports.
4. Strengthen a broad claim through concepts, evidence, causal logic, or narrower scope instead of defensive caveats.
5. Replace vague hedging with the precise source of uncertainty.
6. Distinguish observed, inferred, predicted, and expected statements.
7. Use contrast only when the contrast is part of the argument.
8. Avoid reflexive not-X-but-Y constructions, to be clear, it is worth noting, repeated however, and apology-like framing.
9. Never strengthen prose beyond the graph claim ceiling.

Preserve limitations that affect validity, evidence interpretation, application scope, research design, ethics, safety, law, or correct use. State each necessary limitation once, calmly, normally in Methods, Discussion, or Limitations unless it is essential to the exact sentence.

Before finalizing, remove unnecessary disclaimers, repeated non-claims, excessive hedging, negative framing, and caveats placed in high-impact positions without a direct accuracy need.
